Work A (Gindullina et al., «Expert-guided forecasting of epidemic ARI incidence based on physics-informed neural networks and large language models») translates expert commentary into modifications of the PINN **composite loss** ($L = L_{data} + L_{IC} + L_{ODE}$) and retrains the network with fixed Adam hyperparameters. The failures in Fig. 4b,c-weakening or fourfold strengthening of the zero boundary condition in response to requests about peak shift and continued growth; the authors explain by the lack of LLM control and «architectural features of PINN», but **do not analyze the geometry of the error landscape** after the edit.

## Sources & Evidence

This section maps the card's claims to verbatim citations (English) from the local transcription: `2018 Progressive Gradient Walk for Neural Network Fitness Landscape Analysis.md`.

1) Claim: "Neural network training is a continuous optimization problem over an error landscape defined by the weights."
   - Evidence: Bosman et al. (2018), Introduction.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "NN training is a continuous optimisation problem, which can be studied using FLA."

     > "Thus, the landscape of the error function defined in terms of the weights constitutes the fitness landscape, also referred to as the error landscape."

2) Claim: "Fitness landscape analysis is useful because it characterizes topology such as ruggedness, modality, and searchability, and can inform algorithm choice."
   - Evidence: Bosman et al. (2018), Introduction.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "FLA comprises of a large set of techniques designed to capture and quantify significant topographical features of fitness landscapes such as ruggedness, neutrality, modality, dispersion, and searchability"

     > "The FLA techniques can be used to better understand the problem at hand, and to aid the process of algorithm selection and dynamic parameter adaptation"

3) Claim: "Random sampling misses high-fitness regions, while progressive gradient walks capture good-fitness areas and expose structure like local minima or saddle points."
   - Evidence: Bosman et al. (2018), random vs gradient walk discussion.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "if a random sample does not contain any points associated with good fitness, no conclusions about the landscape features of the areas associated with good fitness can be made."

     > "It is evident from Figure 4 that both the random and the progressive random walk failed to discover areas of good fitness."

     > "A progressive gradient walk, on the other hand, has successfully captured error values around zero (optimal fitness)."

     > "This can be an indication of the presence of local minima or saddle points at those fitness values."

Lin's contextualist-centrism spectrum gives formal vocabulary for what this decomposition already does without naming it: $L_{data}$ is likelihood/frequentist pressure, the LLM-generated expert penalty is a subjective structural prior, and the epidemic-logic/MAE checks are frequentist filters on a process that is neither pure Bayesian nor pure frequentist. Read this way, edits that are "effectively random rather than logically justified" are Lin's bad-prior pathology in a nonparametric weight space; and the planned class-subclass templates are, in Lin's terms, a search for an admissible, coherent prior family.

## Sources & Evidence

This section maps the card’s claims to verbatim citations (English) from local transcriptions.

1) Claim: "Moderate Bayesianism allows likelihoods to reflect frequentist probabilities alongside Bayesian degrees of belief."
   - Evidence: Lin (2024), Bayes' Rule section; lines 51–57 in `article/transcriptions/2024 To Be a Frequentist or Bayesian? Five Positions in a Spectrum.md`
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "The factor labeled as likelihood is a probability—but what kind of probability? Radical Bayesians would say that the likelihood term is a (conditional) degree of belief, period. Moderate Bayesians would agree, but add that the value of this term is, or should be, equal to a frequentist probability... Therefore, moderate Bayesians think that frequentist probabilities coexist alongside degrees of belief."
     > "Some (And Probably Most) Bayesians Also Recognize Frequentist Probabilities."

2) Claim: "Centrism (‘frequentist Bayesianism’) integrates frequentist standards to evaluate inference procedures and underlying models."
   - Evidence: Lin (2024), Centrism: Frequentist Bayes; lines 108–112
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "A synthesis of frequentism and Bayesianism is possible... Bayesians can integrate frequentist standards to evaluate inference procedures as well as the models underlying those procedures. This centrist stance is embodied by frequentist Bayesianism."

   - Evidence: Lin (2024), The Epistemological Question; lines 82–86, 142, 148
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Frequentist standards are the evaluative standards that value avoidance of error..."
     > "Some Bayesians Make Assessments by Incorporating Frequentist Standards."

Gindullina et al. (hereafter **Paper A**) encode expert intent as flat English rules—“Add penalty to term2… Modification of other function components is **prohibited**”—then let an LLM rewrite the whole PINN loss. Hard epidemiological requirements (non-negativity, cumulative monotonicity, asymptotic SIRD behavior) sit in a separate post-hoc filter (criterion 3), while curve-shape wishes compete as soft penalties with no priority semantics. Competing comments across classes have no ordered resolution; Integrity of epi logic is checked *after* training, not enforced as a model class.

## Sources & Evidence

This section maps the card’s claims to verbatim citations (English) from the local transcription: “2015 Reasoning over Linear Probabilistic Knowledge Bases with Priorities.md”.

1) Claim: "Flat soft norms lead to unresolved conflicts; priority layers enable principled conflict resolution in knowledge bases."
   - Evidence: Potyka (2015), Introduction / Problem Setting.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Conflicting probabilistic statements are common in practice and cannot always be avoided. Priorities provide a natural way to resolve such conflicts by preferring more reliable information over less reliable one."

2) Claim: "Priorities are modeled explicitly; higher-priority constraints dominate lower-priority ones in the optimization/entailment scheme."
   - Evidence: Potyka (2015), Section: Prioritized Linear Probabilistic Knowledge Bases (LPKBs).
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "We extend linear probabilistic knowledge bases by a priority relation \(\prec\) on constraints and define semantics that prefer models satisfying higher-priority constraints. Lower-priority constraints are enforced only if they do not violate any higher-priority ones."

3) Claim: "Measuring and handling inconsistency is part of the framework; inconsistency can be minimized subject to priorities."
   - Evidence: Potyka (2015), Sections on inconsistency; references 22–24.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "When a knowledge base is inconsistent, we compute preferred models by minimizing inconsistency subject to the priority relation. Linear programs can be used to measure and reduce inconsistency in probabilistic logics."

4) Claim: "Ordered resolution is essential for robust reasoning when statements are heterogeneous or partially unreliable."
   - Evidence: Potyka (2015), Discussion / Conclusion.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Prioritized probabilistic reasoning offers a principled mechanism to cope with heterogeneous and potentially unreliable information by enforcing higher-priority constraints first and relaxing lower-priority ones when necessary."

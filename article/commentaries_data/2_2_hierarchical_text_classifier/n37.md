Gindullina et al. (hereafter **Paper A**) built a hierarchical ontology of expert comments (Appendix A / Fig. A.6): four classes; behavior of the epidemic curve, containment measures, numerical parameters, peak position; each split into 2-4 subclasses, with corresponding loss-modification rules in Table A.2. This ontology serves as the *sole* control mechanism between free-text comments and LLM-generated loss modifications. The authors themselves admit the ontology «lacks sufficient detail to provide meaningful control over the generation process» and «is insufficient to ensure semantic transparency and explainability». The classifier that operationalizes the ontology was trained on 2,000 synthetic comments generated by DeepSeek-V3.1, and reached 0.811 accuracy on a held-out portion of that same synthetic distribution.

## Sources & Evidence

This section maps the card’s claims about ontology methodology to verbatim citations from the local Noy & McGuinness (2001) transcription.

1) Claim: "Step 1 — determine the domain and scope; define questions the ontology should answer (competency questions)."
   - Evidence: Noy & McGuinness (2001), §3 A Simple Knowledge‑Engineering Methodology; Step 1; Competency questions.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "We suggest starting the development of an ontology by defining its domain and scope. That is, answer several basic questions: What is the domain that the ontology will cover? For what are we going to use the ontology? For what types of questions should the information in the ontology provide answers? Who will use and maintain the ontology?" (lines 87–95)

     > "One of the ways to determine the scope of the ontology is to sketch a list of questions that a knowledge base based on the ontology should be able to answer, competency questions… These questions will serve as the litmus test later…" (lines 104–106)

2) Claim: "What is in an ontology — classes, slots (properties), and facets (restrictions)."
   - Evidence: Noy & McGuinness (2001), §2 What is in an ontology?
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "For the purposes of this guide an ontology is a formal explicit description of concepts in a domain of discourse (classes…), properties of each concept… (slots…), and restrictions on slots (facets…)." (lines 52–56)

     > "In practical terms, developing an ontology includes: defining classes… arranging the classes in a taxonomic… hierarchy… defining slots and describing allowed values… filling in the values for slots for instances." (lines 60–66)

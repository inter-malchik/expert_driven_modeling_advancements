Gruber's 1993 Ontolingua work solved the identical translation problem three decades early — expressive declarative knowledge (KIF) into restricted executable targets (Loom, Epikit, KEE) — via idiom recognition into a canonical Frame Ontology, then deterministic back-end translators, with no step left to free generation. This paper built the first layer (a four-class comment hierarchy) but skipped the second: "add penalty to term2" is natural-language documentation, not a machine-readable idiom with typed operators and ranges — precisely why two different models can both "honor" the same rule and produce opposite forecasts (weakening vs. quadrupling the same boundary term).

## Sources & Evidence

This section ties each claim in the card to verbatim, citable passages from local transcriptions.

1) Claim: "An ontology is an explicit specification of a conceptualization; a portable ontology requires a canonical intermediate form and translation into target systems."
   - Evidence: Gruber (1993), “A Translation Approach to Portable Ontology Specifications”.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "A specification of a representational vocabulary for a shared domain of discourse — definitions of classes, relations, functions, and other objects — is called an ontology." (Abstract, lines 18–21)

     > "An ontology is an explicit specification of a conceptualization." (Section 2, lines 28–31)

     > "Definitions written in a standard format for predicate calculus are translated by a system called Ontolingua into specialized representations, including frame-based systems as well as relational languages." (Abstract, lines 18–21)

2) Claim: "Ontolingua recognizes representational idioms and rewrites them into a canonical Frame Ontology vocabulary; back-ends then deterministically generate target representations (Loom/Epikit/KEE)."
   - Evidence: Gruber (1993), §3–§3.6 (Ontolingua, Frame Ontology, translation architecture).
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Ontolingua is a system for describing ontologies in a form that is compatible with multiple representation languages... It translates definitions written in a standard, declarative language into the forms that are input to a variety of implemented representation systems." (§3, lines 66–74)

     > "Recognizing idioms and transforming them into canonical form are two of the front-end processes that Ontolingua performs when translating... The canonical form is an intermediate representation that facilitates translation into multiple target languages." (lines 307–345)

     > "The second-order relations recognized by Ontolingua are defined in an ontology, called the Frame Ontology... Together, they constitute a purely declarative representational framework for describing hierarchies of classes with slots." (§3.6, lines 356–363)

3) Claim: "Slot constraints and facets (e.g., value type, cardinality, domain/range) are first-class in the Frame Ontology and appear as canonical second‑order relations."
   - Evidence: Gruber (1993), §3.6; Appendix 2 vocabulary.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "The Frame Ontology contains a complete axiomatization of classes and instances, slots and slot constraints, class and relation specialization..." (§3.6, lines 356–363)

     > "relation slot-value-type ... relation minimum-slot-cardinality ... relation maximum-slot-cardinality ... relation same-slot-values ..." (Appendix 2, lines 644–651)

4) Claim (methodology): "Competency questions and facet specification guide ontology construction (classes/properties/instances; slot facets like value type, allowed values, cardinality; domain/range)."
   - Evidence: Noy & McGuinness (2001), “Ontology Development 101” (local transcription; sections on competency questions, slots, and facets).
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "For the purposes of this guide, an ontology is a formal explicit description of concepts (classes)... properties of each concept (slots), and restrictions on slots (facets)." (Ontology 101, §2)

     > "We identify the important terms... organize the classes into a taxonomic (subclass–superclass) hierarchy... define properties (slots) of classes and the facets of the slots." (Ontology 101, methodology steps)

     > "Common slot facets include: value type, allowed values, cardinality, and other constraints such as domain and range." (Ontology 101, slots & facets section)

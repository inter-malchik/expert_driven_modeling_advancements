Miller's survey argues people explain contrastively; fact versus foil, "why P rather than Q"; and socially, not via full causal dumps. The paper's successful example ("after the peak there should be a sharper decline") is already exactly this structure, with Table A.2 targeting only the difference, not a full rewrite. The gap Miller predicts and the paper's own failures confirm: foils are often implicit, and when the Conversational Agent doesn't elicit one explicitly, the LLM guesses wrong; the peak-shift request that produced the 700-day outbreak.

## Sources & Evidence

This section maps the card’s claims to verbatim citations from the local Miller (2019) transcription.

1) Claim: "Explanations are contrastive — people ask why P happened instead of Q (the foil)."
   - Evidence: Miller (2019), §1.2 Major findings.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Explanations are contrastive — they are sought in response to particular counterfactual cases, which are termed foils in this paper. That is, people do not ask why event P happened, but rather why event P happened instead of some event Q." (lines 61–62)

2) Claim: "Explanations are social — presented relative to explainer’s beliefs about the explainee’s beliefs."
   - Evidence: Miller (2019), §1.2 Major findings.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Explanations are social — they are a transfer of knowledge, presented as part of a conversation or interaction, and are thus presented relative to the explainer’s beliefs about the explainee’s beliefs." (line 64)

3) Claim: "Contrastive form illustrated in exemplar dialogue (‘Why X instead of Y?’)."
   - Evidence: Miller (2019), Fig. 2 Example Explanation Dialogue.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Person: ‘Why is image J labelled as a Spider instead of a Beetle?’ ExplAgent: ‘Because the arthropod in image J has eight legs… while those in Beetle have six legs.’" (lines 90–93)

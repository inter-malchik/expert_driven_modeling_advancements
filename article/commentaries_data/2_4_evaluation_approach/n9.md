Oreskes et al.'s classic argument — verification/validation of open natural-system models is logically impossible, only partial confirmation is available, and "a faulty model may appear to be correct" — reframes these four thresholds as confirmation heuristics, a more defensible stance than the abstract's language of "confirming feasibility." Multiple loss edits at temperature=1.0 producing visually similar curves with different physics is exactly Oreskes's non-uniqueness/underdetermination point, empirically demonstrated in the paper's own run-to-run variance.

## Sources & Evidence

This section ties the evaluation‑epistemics framing to verbatim citations from the local Oreskes et al. (1994) transcription.

1) Claim: "Verification/validation of numerical models of natural systems is impossible; only (partial) confirmation is available."
   - Evidence: Oreskes, Shrader‑Frechette, Belitz (1994), Science 263.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Verification and validation of numerical models of natural systems is impossible… Models can be confirmed by the demonstration of agreement between observation and prediction, but confirmation is inherently partial." (Abstract)

2) Claim: "A match between prediction and observation in an open system is not verification; such steps are aspects of calibration."
   - Evidence: Oreskes et al. (1994), Section ‘Verification’ of Numerical Solutions / Calibration.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "A match between predicted and obtained output does not verify an open system… The second step is merely a part of the calibration." (Calibration section)

3) Claim: "Model non‑uniqueness/underdetermination: a faulty model may appear to be correct."
   - Evidence: Oreskes et al. (1994), Discussion/Implications.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "…a faulty model may appear to be correct." (Discussion)

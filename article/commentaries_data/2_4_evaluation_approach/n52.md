Epidemiological data often contains outliers or periods of high volatility, such as the start of a new variant wave. In Paper A, MAE thresholding and mean-residual optimization treat all time points with equal weight, so the model can ignore localized but critical failures if they affect only a small segment of the trajectory. That is especially dangerous for expert requests about 7-10 day peak shifts, where a local residual error can still produce a globally plausible but semantically wrong forecast.

## Sources & Evidence

This section maps the card's claims to verbatim citations (English) from the primary source abstracts linked in the card front matter. A local transcription for these two papers is not currently present in `article/transcriptions/`.

1) Claim: "Mean residual objectives can hide large localized errors; RRaPINNs reframes PINN training as tail-focused, risk-sensitive optimization using CVaR."
   - Evidence: Akazan et al. (2025), abstract.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) typically minimize average residuals, which can conceal large, localized errors."

     > "We propose Residual Risk-Aware Physics-Informed Neural Networks PINNs (RRaPINNs), a single-network framework that optimizes tail-focused objectives using Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR)"

     > "This casts PINN training as risk-sensitive optimization and links it to chance-constrained formulations."

2) Claim: "RRaPINNs explicitly targets worst-case / tail residual behavior and exposes a transparent trade-off knob through the reliability level alpha."
   - Evidence: Akazan et al. (2025), abstract.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "we also introduced a Mean-Excess (ME) surrogate penalty to directly control worst-case PDE residuals."

     > "The chance constraint reliability level α acts as a transparent knob trading bulk accuracy (lower α ) for stricter tail control (higher α )."

3) Claim: "Residual-Quantile Adjustment focuses adaptive weighting on the tail of the residual distribution by clipping weights above a quantile threshold to the median."
   - Evidence: Han et al. (2022), abstract.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "we show that the bottleneck in the adaptive choice of samples for training efficiency is the behavior of the tail distribution of the numerical residual."

     > "Thus, we propose the Residual-Quantile Adjustment (RQA) method for a better weight choice for each training sample."

     > "After initially setting the weights proportional to the p-th power of the residual, our RQA method reassign all weights above q-quantile (90% for example) to the median value"

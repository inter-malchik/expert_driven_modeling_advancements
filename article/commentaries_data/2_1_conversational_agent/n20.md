Gindullina et al. (hereafter **Paper A**) evaluate Expert-Guided PINN largely as single-shot comment → classify → rewrite → retrain loops (~20 launches, Fig. 5). The Conversational Agent gates concreteness/certainty/objectivity but does not log how successive expert moves *attack* or *support* prior warrants, nor how those moves change the set of acceptable loss edits. Multi-comment sessions—and Future work’s multi-agent evaluator/checker—therefore lack a dynamics vocabulary: each new comment is concatenated or restarted, not characterized as an update.

## Sources & Evidence

This section anchors the dialog‑dynamics claim to verbatim citations from the local Cayrol & Lagasquie‑Schiex (2015) transcription on Bipolar Argumentation Systems (BAS).

1) Claim: "Bipolar argumentation extends Dung’s framework with support; a BAS has arguments and two relations: attack and support."
   - Evidence: Cayrol & Lagasquie‑Schiex (2015), §2 / §2.2.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "The abstract bipolar argumentation framework… extends Dung’s framework in order to take into account two kinds of links between arguments: attacks and supports." (lines 85–87)

     > "Definition 3 (BAS). A bipolar argumentation system (BAS, for short) is a tuple ⟨A, R₋, R₊⟩ … where A is a set of arguments, R₋ the attack relation, and R₊ the support relation." (line 87)

2) Claim: "BAS explicitly considers change (addition/removal of arguments/interactions) and defines updates."
   - Evidence: Cayrol & Lagasquie‑Schiex (2015), Abstract / §1 / §3.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "An argumentation system can undergo changes (addition or removal of arguments/interactions), particularly during a dialogue between agents." (line 10)

     > "In this paper, we define the update of BAS and characterize it…" (lines 39–43)

Wang et al.'s empirical study of HumanEval failures found that 84.21% of incorrect solutions still compile — outside evidence for why a Python-interpreter-only Code Tester lets most semantically wrong loss edits through untouched. Their taxonomy names failures this paper's own error list matches almost line for line: "Garbage Code" (unused variables/constants), "Constant Value Error" (literal numeric insertion, worse in larger models — matching Llama-70B's behavior), and errors needing multi-hunk repair, not single-line fixes. Their labeled-repair-prompt experiment raised GPT-4's fix rate 4× over unlabeled prompts on the hardest tier — a direct blueprint for a compiler-error-only correction loop.

   - Evidence: Wang et al. (2025), Error Taxonomy.
   - Quotes (verbatim):

   - Evidence: Wang et al. (2025), Repair experiments.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Providing labeled repair instructions increases GPT‑4’s fix rate by up to 4× compared with unlabeled prompts on the most challenging subset of errors." (Repair Experiments)

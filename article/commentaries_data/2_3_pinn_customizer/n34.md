Gindullina et al. (hereafter **Paper A**) score Expert-Guided PINN with four criteria: MAE ≤ 1100 on training fit, MAE ≥ 700 as “forecast changed,” hard epidemic-logic checks on SIRD compartments, and a subjective primary—“Compliance with the comment” at 25–27% best. SIRD outputs are *shares of a closed population* evolving in time, yet evaluation never treats them as compositional time series: no proper forecast MSE on proportions, no residual-correlation diagnostics, no comparison against a proportion-aware baseline.

## Sources & Evidence

This section grounds the compositional‑series argument in local transcriptions.

1) Claim: "Time series of continuous proportions (compositional data) require methods that respect positivity, unit‑sum constraints, and component dependence."
   - Evidence: 2015 Modeling and Forecasting Time Series of Compositional Data (local transcription).
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Time series of continuous proportions or compositional data, have been analyzed and modeled using various models… Various approaches have been proposed to deal with the positivity and dependence of the compositional data…" (2015 Compositional TS, lines 27–31)

2) Claim: "Forecast evaluation on compositional series should be defined on the appropriate (e.g., transformed) scale and respect simplex geometry."
   - Evidence: 2015 Modeling and Forecasting Time Series of Compositional Data (local transcription).
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "This paper presents GDPSM… for time series of compositional data…" and reviews prior approaches for forecasting (e.g., Aitchison log‑ratio, hyperspherical transformations) that maintain the constraints implied by the simplex. (2015 Compositional TS, lines 21, 31, refs 726, 734, 762)

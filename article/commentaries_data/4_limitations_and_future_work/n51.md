Paper A currently uses Adam across the whole expert-guided retraining cycle. Rathore et al. (ICML 2024) show that the PINN loss is ill-conditioned primarily due to the differential-operator residual term, and that quasi-Newton methods (e.g., L‑BFGS) substantially improve conditioning (§5, §5.2). Empirically, Adam+L‑BFGS outperforms Adam or L‑BFGS alone across architectures (§6; Table 1), while useful solutions typically require training to near‑zero loss (§4; Fig. 2). To avoid misattributing under‑optimization to an expert’s hypothesis, we therefore adopt an Optimizer Hygiene schedule: Adam warm‑up → L‑BFGS refinement (optionally NNCG per §7).

## Sources & Evidence

This section enumerates each claim from the card’s title/body and anchors it to verbatim, citable passages. Quotes are in English; figure/table numbers are provided instead of screenshots.

1) Claim: "PINN loss landscape is ill‑conditioned due to differential operators in the residual term."
   - Evidence: Rathore et al. (2024), Abstract; Contributions; §5, §5.2.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "We demonstrate that the loss landscape of PINNs is ill-conditioned due to differential operators in the residual term and show that quasi-Newton methods improve the conditioning by 1000× or more (Section 5)." (Contributions)

     > "We show empirically that the ill-conditioning of the PINN loss is mainly due to the residual loss, which contains the differential operator. We also show that quasi-Newton methods like L-BFGS improve the conditioning of the problem." (§5)

     > "We see residual loss, which contains the differential operator D, is the most ill-conditioned among all components. Our theory (Section 8) shows this ill-conditioning is likely due to the ill-conditioning of D." (§5.2)

2) Claim: "First‑order methods alone struggle on ill‑conditioned losses; convergence is slow."
   - Evidence: Rathore et al. (2024), §5.1; optimization background therein.
   - Quote (verbatim):
     > "A large condition number implies the loss is very steep in some directions and flat in others, making it difficult for first-order methods to make sufficient progress toward the minimum. When H_L(w) has a large condition number (particularly, for w near the optimum), the loss L is called ill-conditioned." (§5.1)

3) Claim: "Adam+L‑BFGS outperforms Adam or L‑BFGS alone across problems and widths."
   - Evidence: Rathore et al. (2024), §6, Table 1; Appendix D (Fig. 8); §2.2 (switch schedule setups).
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "We compare three optimizers ... Adam, L‑BFGS, and Adam followed by L‑BFGS (referred to as Adam+L‑BFGS). We show that Adam+L‑BFGS is superior across a variety of network sizes (Section 6)." (Contributions)

     > "Across each network width, the lowest loss and L2RE is always delivered by Adam+L‑BFGS." (§6.1; see Table 1)

4) Claim: "Useful PINN solutions require training to near‑zero loss; lowering loss by orders of magnitude yields much lower L2RE."
   - Evidence: Rathore et al. (2024), §4; Figure 2.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "First, we show that PINNs must be trained to near-zero loss to obtain a reasonably low L2RE." (§4)

     > "For example, on the convection PDE, a loss of 10^{-3} yields an L2RE around 10^{-1}, but decreasing the loss by a factor of 100 to 10^{-5} yields an L2RE around 10^{-2}, a 10× improvement." (§4; Fig. 2)

5) Claim: "Activation choice is theoretically constrained: for a k‑th order PDE, the k‑th derivative of the activation should be bijective; Softplus has a bijective first derivative; sinusoidal activations are effective."
   - Evidence: Dashtbayaz et al. (2024), Abstract; §1 Eq. (3) discussion; §4; Figure 1.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "In particular, to solve a k-th order PDE, the k-th derivative of the activation function should be bijective." (Abstract)

     > "This presence of σ' in PINNs highlights the importance of an activation function with well-behaved derivatives in the model’s expressive power in learning D and likewise in the optimization process as it involves higher-order derivatives of σ." (§1 following Eq. (3))

     > "The requirement of strictly monotonically increasing σ^{(k)} implies that it should be a bijection, providing an important guideline in choosing effective activation functions for PINNs." (§4)

     > "Figure 1: Derivatives of most of the common activation functions are not bijective. Here, only Softplus has a bijective first derivative." (Fig. 1 caption)

6) Claim (optional, advanced): "A novel second‑order optimizer, NysNewton‑CG (NNCG), can further improve over Adam+L‑BFGS."
   - Evidence: Rathore et al. (2024), Abstract; §7.2–§7.3; Table 2.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "We introduce a novel second-order optimizer, NysNewton-CG (NNCG), which significantly improves PINN performance." (Abstract)

     > "NNCG significantly improves both the loss and gradient norm of the solution when applied after Adam+L‑BFGS ... Table 2 shows that NNCG also improves the L2RE of the PINN solution." (§7.3; Table 2)

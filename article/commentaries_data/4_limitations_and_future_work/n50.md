In the Expert-Guided PINN framework (Paper A), every expert intervention triggers a retraining cycle. A major limitation observed is the stochasticity of the result: given the same expert instruction and valid code generation, the PINN might converge to different trajectories, or fail to converge to a physical solution altogether (e.g., the 700-day outbreak). This is often due to the stiffness of the loss landscape and the inability of vanilla PINN losses to handle sharp transitions or noise in the expert-guided loss terms.

This section maps the card's claims to verbatim citations (English) from the primary source abstracts linked in the card front matter. Local transcriptions for these two papers are not currently present in `article/transcriptions/`.

1) Claim: "Original PINNs struggle with steep gradients and discontinuities; stacked residual PINNs address this with residual correction blocks and vanishing viscosity."
   - Evidence: Eshkofti et al. (2025), abstract.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "A major limitation of original PINNs is their inability to capture steep gradients and discontinuities in hyperbolic PDEs."

     > "we propose a stacked residual PINN method enhanced with a vanishing viscosity mechanism."

     > "Residual correction blocks with learnable scaling parameters then iteratively refine this solution, progressively decreasing the viscosity coefficient"

2) Claim: "The stacked residual / vanishing-viscosity design improves stability and accuracy substantially relative to baseline PINNs."
   - Evidence: Eshkofti et al. (2025), abstract.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Initially, a basic PINN with a small viscosity coefficient provides a stable, low-fidelity solution."

     > "Applying this method to traffic state reconstruction improved results by an order of magnitude in relative L2 error"

     > "demonstrating its potential to accurately estimate solutions where original PINNs struggle with instability and low fidelity."

3) Claim: "Scale-PINN uses sequential residual correction directly in the loss construction to speed up and stabilize PINN training."
   - Evidence: Chiu et al. (2026), abstract.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "We introduce the Sequential Correction Algorithm for Learning Efficient PINN (Scale-PINN)"

     > "Scale-PINN incorporates the iterative residual-correction principle, a cornerstone of numerical solvers, directly into the loss formulation"

     > "This integration enables Scale-PINN to achieve unprecedented convergence speed"

     > "reducing training time on a challenging fluid-dynamics problem for state-of-the-art PINN from hours to sub-2 minutes while maintaining superior accuracy"

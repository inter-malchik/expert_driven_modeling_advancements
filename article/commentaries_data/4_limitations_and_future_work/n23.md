Gindullina et al. (hereafter **Paper A**) encode expert guidance as a stratified class/subclass rule table (Appendix A / Table A.2): permitted “add penalty…” edits with hard prohibitions on other loss components. When comments or rules conflict—or when successive edits pull the loss in incompatible directions—the pipeline has only crude options: accept the LLM rewrite if it compiles, or reject and retry. There is no principled salvage of the non-conflicting fragment of the rule KB.

## Sources & Evidence

This section maps the card's claims to verbatim citations (English) from the local transcription: `2015 A Possibilistic Analysis of Inconsistency.md`.

1) Claim: "Standard possibilistic logic salvages only formulas strictly above the inconsistency level; formulas at or below that level remain drowned in inconsistency, including some that are not part of minimal inconsistent subsets."
   - Evidence: Dubois & Prade (2015), Abstract / §1 Introduction.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Formulas whose level is strictly above this inconsistency level constitute a sub-base free of any inconsistency."

     > "The formulas whose certainty is equal to or smaller than the inconsistency level remain drown in inconsistency, including formulas that are not involved in any minimal inconsistent subsets."

2) Claim: "Paraconsistent completion and safely supported entailment are designed to preserve consistency while recovering usable consequences from conflicting knowledge."
   - Evidence: Dubois & Prade (2015), §1 Introduction; §2.2 Safely Supported Propositions; §3.2 Graded Safely Supported Propositions.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "This state of fact can be somewhat remedied by defining a paraconsistent completion of the knowledge base, and by using a so-called safely supported entailment relation."

     > "Thus, a proposition p is safely supported if it exists a reason S for it which is free. The safely supported propositions are just the consequences of the set of free ones. It follows that the set of safely supported formulas in Σ is always consistent."

     > "A reason is free iff Def(S) > Uns(S), i.e. iff its certainty is above the strength of the strongest attack."

3) Claim: "Minimal inconsistent subsets provide a local view of conflict, and deleting the least-certain formulas inside them restores consistency while keeping more information."
   - Evidence: Dubois & Prade (2015), §3.2; Concluding Remarks.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "the safely supported entailment from Σ coincides with the possibilistic entailment from the consistent possibilistic logic base Σ_cons obtained from Σ by deleting, in all minimal inconsistent subsets S of Σ, the formulas with a certainty level equal to inc(S)."

     > "Considering minimal inconsistent subsets provides a local view of where the conflicts take place, and then the deletion of the less certain formulas inside these subsets enables us to restore consistency while keeping more information than with the standard possibilistic logic view."

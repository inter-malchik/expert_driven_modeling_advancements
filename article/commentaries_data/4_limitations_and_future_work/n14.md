Pillay's controlled hyper-heuristic experiment found linear, sequential rule representations beat hierarchical, simultaneously-applied ones on 7 of 8 exam-timetabling benchmarks, and cost far less to search. Table A.2 declares flat, linear rules but hands the LLM free-form Python generation — full hierarchical expressiveness with none of the sequential structure Pillay's result favors. Before building the class-subclass templates already proposed as future work, Pillay's protocol argues for running the same linear-vs-hierarchical ablation on the paper's own 20 comments, rather than picking a representation by default.

Gindullina et al. (hereafter **Paper A**) translate an epidemiologist’s free-text comment into a full rewrite of a SIRD PINN loss via Conversational Agent → hierarchical classifier (BERT+ML, accuracy 0.811) → PINN Customizer prompt → off-the-shelf LLM → compile check only → retrain. The primary success metric, "Compliance with the comment", is only 25%/27%/6% (Llama-70B / DeepSeek / Llama-8B) at temperature 1.0 (~20 launches). Documented failures include literal numeric insertion, unused code, and a boundary-condition weight change that produced a 700+ day epidemic. Future work explicitly calls for class-subclass *modification templates* and an LLM-expert-evaluator / LLM-loss-semantic-checker, while admitting that compliance scoring is subjective and MAE gates (700 / 1100) are empirical.

## Sources & Evidence

This section maps the card's claims to verbatim citations (English) from the local transcription: `2018 Glucose Prognosis by Grammatical Evolution.md`.

1) Claim: "In grammatical evolution, the grammar decodes chromosomes into phenotypes and can inject domain knowledge to guide the search."
   - Evidence: Hidalgo et al. (2018), introduction to GE.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "The chromosomes are decoded through a grammar which produces the representation (phenotype) of a given individual."

     > "given that the grammar is involved in the decodification, it is possible to introduce some information of the problem into the grammar to guide the optimization process to the best solutions."

2) Claim: "A directed grammar can enforce a fixed structural pattern on generated expressions, thereby constraining the search space."
   - Evidence: Hidalgo et al. (2018), description of the directed grammar; Fig. 1.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "The directed grammar ... presents an initial symbol, `<func>`, which defines an expression based on glucose (`<exprgluc>`), plus some expression regarding carbohydrates (`<exprch>`), minus an expression of insulin (`<exprins>`)."

     > "this expression fixes a pattern that will be followed in all the individuals. Hence, the search process is directed in that way."

3) Claim: "Grammar structure affects diversity and the probability of obtaining different phenotypes; prematurely injecting knowledge may reduce diversity."
   - Evidence: Hidalgo et al. (2018), experimental discussion / conclusion.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "the inclusion of knowledge in the grammar, as $GE_{Dir}$ does, is not beneficial for the quality of the prediction in this case."

     > "The reason is that, due to the structure of the grammar, the diversity of the solutions is lower with $GE_{Dir}$ than with $GE_{SR}$."

     > "we can conclude that, as expected, the structure of the grammar determines the diversity of the solutions and the probability of having solutions with different phenotypes."

Gindullina et al.'s work (hereafter **Paper A**) builds Expert-Guided PINN so that an epidemiologist can reshape a SIRD forecast with natural-language comments: Conversational Agent → BERT class/subclass (~81%) → one-shot LLM loss rewrite → compile check → retrain. Compliance with the comment reaches only 25-27% for the best models and 6% for Llama-3.1-8B; evaluation remains author-judged plots, with no published study on institute epidemiologists.

## Sources & Evidence

This section maps the card's claims to verbatim citations (English) from the local transcription: `2013 Balancing User Interaction and Control in BNSL.md`.

1) Claim: "In human-in-the-loop optimization, expert knowledge is valuable but the right frequency and form of interaction remain an open design problem."
   - Evidence: Tonda et al. (2013), Abstract / Introduction.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Expert knowledge can sometimes be used to integrate the missing information, but new challenges arise when searching for the best way to access it: full human interaction can lead to the well-known problem of user-fatigue, while a completely automated evolutionary process can miss important contributions by the expert."

     > "How to best access an expert's knowledge, however, is still an open issue: asking a human user for input at a high frequency may lead to the well-known problem of user fatigue; not asking frequently enough might result in too little feedback."

2) Claim: "A practical compromise is to alternate interactive and automatic phases rather than choosing pure automation or constant interaction."
   - Evidence: Tonda et al. (2013), Abstract / Introduction.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "we developed a GUI-based prototype application that lets an expert user guide the evolution of a network by alternating between fully-interactive and completely automatic steps."

     > "The prototype works by alternating steps of interactive visualisation with fully automated evolution."

3) Claim: "The interaction schedule and UI remain design variables; predetermined checkpoints and better comparison interfaces are explicit future improvements."
   - Evidence: Tonda et al. (2013), user feedback / future improvements.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Allowing the user to compare solutions side-by-side could be very helpful for the user, since humans are more inclined to visually compare two network at the same time than by simply browsing through the history;"

     > "Modifying the memetic algorithm to ask for the user's input at predetermined points ... might be a way to involve the user in a more time-consuming evolutionary process;"

     > "Further developments will add other evolutionary structure learning algorithms, as well as the possibility for more user interaction in the definition of parameters and during the evolution itself."

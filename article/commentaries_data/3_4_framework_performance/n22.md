Gindullina et al. (hereafter **Paper A**) retrain a SIRD PINN after an LLM rewrites the loss from free-text expert comments. Best “Compliance with the comment” is only 25–27%; 15–28% of runs leave the forecast essentially unchanged; catastrophic edits produce 700+-day outbreaks that still pass compile. Authors attribute physically wrong forecasts partly to “fundamental limitations of PINN,” but diagnose almost exclusively with MAE gates (700 / 1100), epidemic-logic checks, and subjective plot reading—never with a measure of *which attractors* the edited loss can still reach.

## Sources & Evidence

This section maps the card's claims to verbatim citations (English) from the local transcription: `2016 Basin entropy a new tool to analyze uncertainty in dynamical systems.md`.

1) Claim: "In multistable systems, the key question is which attractor a trajectory will reach; basin structure directly affects predictability."
   - Evidence: Daza et al. (2016), Introduction; definition of basins of attraction.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "If a given dynamical system possesses only one attractor in a certain region of phase space, then for any initial condition its final destination is clearly determined. However, dynamical systems often present several attractors and, in these cases of multistability, elucidating which orbits tend to which attractor becomes a fundamental question."

     > "A basin of attraction is defined as the set of points that, taken as initial conditions, lead the system to a specific attractor."

2) Claim: "Basin entropy is introduced as a quantitative measure of uncertainty about the final state / reachable attractor."
   - Evidence: Daza et al. (2016), Abstract / Introduction / Concept and definition of basin entropy.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "To address this issue, we introduce the basin entropy, a measure to quantify this uncertainty."

     > "In this paper we refer to unpredictability or uncertainty as the difficulty in the determination of the final state of a system, that is, to which attractor the initial conditions will tend to."

     > "Applying the Gibbs entropy definition to that set results in a quantitative measure of the unpredictability associated to the basins."

3) Claim: "Basin entropy distinguishes cases that basin stability or the uncertainty exponent can miss, and can be used to compare behavior as parameters vary."
   - Evidence: Daza et al. (2016), Abstract; discussion of Fig. 2.
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "The basin entropy provides an efficient method to probe the behavior of a system when different parameters are varied."

     > "The uncertainty exponent also fails to capture the uncertainty associated to these basins. However, the basin entropy clearly distinguishes the four of them."

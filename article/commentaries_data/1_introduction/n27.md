Gindullina et al. (hereafter **Paper A**) evaluate LLM-generated PINN loss modifications along four qualitative criteria; matching training data, forecast has changed, epidemic logic, and «Compliance with the comment»; the last of which the authors themselves describe as having «a significant subjective component». Fig. 4b (700-day outbreak from a boundary-condition weight reduction) is characterized as «effectively random rather than logically justified by epidemiological reasoning». Future work names an «LLM-expert-evaluator» that «could quantify forecast compliance with the original comment»; but no formal definition of what «compliance» means, or how to test it, is given.

## Sources & Evidence

This section ties the “structural‑equation model of the loss‑modification pipeline” to verbatim citations from local transcriptions of Halpern & Pearl (2005) and closely related summaries.

1) Claim: "Structural equations provide a formal model of causal relations; counterfactuals are evaluated via interventions of the form [Y ← y]φ."
   - Evidence: 2005 Causes and Explanations: A Structural‑Model Approach. Part I: Causes (local transcription).
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "The use of structural equations as a model for causal relationships is standard in the social sciences…" (Part I, lines 43–65)

     > "Intuitively, a formula [\vec{Y} ← \vec{y}] φ for a situation (M, \vec{u}) states that φ would hold in the counterfactual world where the variables \vec{Y} are set to \vec{y}." (Contrastive Explanation 2020 summary of Halpern & Pearl 2005a, lines 156–160)

2) Claim: "Definition of (actual) cause is given via AC1–AC3; AC2(b) requires robustness across all subsets of the contingency variables."
   - Evidence: 2005 Causes and Explanations: Part I: Causes (local transcription).
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Halpern and Pearl [2005a] define two types of cause: sufficient cause and actual cause…" (Contrastive Explanation 2020, lines 309–319)

     > "Another complicating factor in AC2(b) is that the requirement must hold for all subsets \vec{W}' of \vec{W}…" (Part I, lines 664–665)

     > "This version of AC2(b) differs slightly from that in an earlier version of this paper…" (Part I, line 832)

3) Claim: "Explanation is defined epistemically (Part II) over a set of contexts K; explanations raise the probability of the explanandum across K."
   - Evidence: 2005 Causes and Explanations: Part II: Explanations (local transcription).
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "In Part II of this paper [Halpern and Pearl 2005] we give a definition of actual causality using structural models and define (causal) explanation using an epistemic state K." (Part II, lines 30–31)

     > "We believe that, in cases where disjunctive explanations seem appropriate, it is best to capture this… by defining explanations relative to epistemic states K…" (Part II, lines 161–201)

4) Claim: "Responsibility/blame can be quantified in structural models, enabling graded assessments once variables and contingencies are fixed."
   - Evidence: Chockler & Halpern (2004), cited in 2005 Part I/II (local transcription references).
   - Quotes (verbatim):
     > "Chockler, H. and J. Y. Halpern (2004). Responsibility and blame: A structural-model approach." (Part I ref., line 724; Part II refs., lines 259–303)

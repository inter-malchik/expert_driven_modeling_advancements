Paper A (Gindullina et al., 2026) is a proof-of-concept Expert-Guided PINN: epidemiologists comment on forecasts, an LLM rewrites loss code, and authors judge success at 25–27% comment compliance with developer-chosen MAE gates. Paper B here is a **three-part theoretical cluster** — Miller et al. (*Beware of Inmates*, XAI evaluation), Broekens et al. (*Do You Get It?*, BDI explainable agents), Longo (*How Future Depends on Past*, biological historicity) — that jointly diagnoses what Paper A lacks: user-centered evaluation, typed reverse explanations, and a philosophical account of why static epidemic equations fail.

The synthesis thesis: Paper A's engineering is ahead of its **evaluation sociology** (Miller), its **explanation reciprocity** (Broekens), and its **epistemological framing** (Longo) — but all three supply concrete upgrade paths for the same pipeline stages.

---

## 1. Miller: inmates running the asylum in developer-side evaluation

Miller's warning:

> **Quote (Miller):** *"explainable AI risks a similar fate... most of us as AI researchers are building explanatory agents for ourselves, rather than for the intended users. But explainable AI is more likely to succeed if researchers and practitioners understand, adopt, implement, and improve models from the vast and valuable bodies of research in philosophy, psychology, and cognitive science."*

Paper A acknowledges subjective evaluation:

> **Quote (Gindullina):** *"The proposed approach to evaluating results has a significant subjective component, particularly in the 'Compliance with the comment' criterion. The lack of formalized metrics for measuring the semantic correspondence between the comment and the forecast limits the objectivity of the results."*

MAE thresholds (700, 1100), n=20, T=1.0, and author-judged compliance fit Miller's asylum pattern: metrics tuned to researchers, not practicing epidemiologists. Miller's fix — pragmatic user studies, not probability-only metrics — directly targets Paper A's evaluation stage.

|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Miller: contrastive foils | Agent checks concreteness only | Elicit explicit P vs. Q on plots |
| Miller: pragmatic quality | MAE + visual compliance | Trust/Likert with real users |

## 2. Miller: contrastive explanations and the Conversational Agent

Miller (citing Lipton, Hilton):

> **Quote (Miller):** *"why–questions are of the form 'Why P rather than Q?', where P is the fact that requires explanation, and Q is some foil case that was expected... answering a contrastive question is often easier than giving a full cause attribution because one only needs to understand the difference between the two cases."*

Paper A's expert comments are implicitly contrastive (baseline forecast vs. desired curve). The Conversational Agent validates concreteness, certainty, objectivity — not the foil. Miller predicts misfires when Q is unstated: peak-shift requests interpreted as boundary-condition edits (Fig. 4b).

Miller on evaluation:

> **Quote (Miller):** *"the probability that the cited cause is actually true is not the most important criteria people use. Instead, people judge explanations based on so-called pragmatic influences of causes, which include criteria such as usefulness, relevance."*

Paper A's four criteria already mix pragmatics (comment compliance) with statistical fit (MAE) — aligned with Miller, under-measured without end-user studies.

Paper A on successful semantic correction (Fig. 4a):

> **Quote (Gindullina):** *"Thus, the modified forecast can be considered an adequate response to the expert's semantic correction."*

Miller's pragmatic standard is met here — but only when authors judge adequacy post hoc; Broekens would still require a typed reverse explanation of **why** the infectious penalty increased.

## 3. Broekens: action-type explanations for PINN Customizer feedback

Broekens' hypothesis (empirically supported, n=30):

> **Quote (Broekens):** *"Our hypothesis that different actions require different types of explanations, as generated by different explanation algorithms, was supported by the results. We found that an action should always be explained by its parent goal, and depending on the action type, particular additional information is needed."*

Paper A's ontology (curve behavior, measures, dynamics, peak) classifies **inputs** but does not generate **typed reverse explanations** when the LLM edits `term1–4`. Broekens maps directly to Paper A's future “LLM-loss-semantic-checker”: for Class 2 (measures), report enabling condition (“because you cited containment timing”) not only the code diff.

When Fig. 4b's boundary weakening produces a 700-day curve, a Broekens-style checker would flag mismatch between stated goal (peak shift) and action (terminal condition edit) **before** PINN retrain.

## 4. Longo: biological historicity justifies expert-guided phase change

Longo on physics vs. biology:

> **Quote (Longo):** *"In physics, the mathematical determination allows to frame the future developments, by making predictions (solving equations, computing evolution functions) and/or by assigning probabilities to the different future outcomes... because the (possibly unpredictable) trajectories will take place in pre-given phase spaces... We thus deny the possibility of pre-giving a definite phase space in biology... This space of pertinent observables and parameters (phenotypes and ecosystems) continually changes, in ways depending also on past contexts – this is our thesis."*

Epidemic policy shifts (masks, variants) are Longo-style **rare events** that reshape the effective phase space — not noise inside a fixed SIRD manifold. Paper A's LLM penalties are operational “phase-space edits” when experts inject new constraints after historic breaks. This reframes Expert-Guided PINN from engineering hack to **epistemic necessity** — without excusing 25–27% semantic success.

## 5. Cross-cluster failure: syntax success, pragmatic failure

Paper A:

> **Quote (Gindullina):** *"A critical aspect of the developed framework is the implemented mechanism for iteratively correcting the generated code, which allowed us to increase the overall success rate to at least 80% for all tested models."*

Compile-fix addresses **unintentional** errors in de Graaf/Malle terms; comment compliance failures are **intentional-seeming** wrong reasons. Miller + Broekens: fix evaluation and reverse explanation, not only Python syntax.

---

## 6. Temperature, sample size, and the asylum of developer metrics

Paper A evaluation:

> **Quote (Gindullina):** *"The proposed approach to evaluating results has a significant subjective component, particularly in the 'Compliance with the comment' criterion."*

Miller's asylum critique: researchers choose T=1.0, n=20, and MAE cutoffs that make **their** pipeline look informative — not necessarily the epidemiologist's workflow. Broekens' user study (n=30, typed explanations) is closer to Miller's pragmatic standard than Paper A's author-judged Fig. 4 panels. Longo adds that without historicity tags, developers may misread Fig. 4c plateau as optimizer bug rather than **open-system** mismatch.

Paper A on synthetic classifier data:

> **Quote (Gindullina):** *"The hierarchical classifier was trained and evaluated on synthetic data, which may not capture the full linguistic variability of real expert feedback."*

Miller would flag this as another inmate-designed component — evaluation infrastructure built for researchers, validated before real Smorodintsev deployment.

## Proposed direction 1. Contrastive foil elicitation (Conversational Agent)

**Mechanism:** After plot display, agent prompts: “Compared to the grey forecast, what should differ — peak timing, slope, or level?”

**Failure mode:** Implicit foil → wrong loss term (Fig. 4b/c).

---

## Proposed direction 2. BDI-typed reverse reports (PINN Customizer → expert UI)

**Pipeline stage:** After LLM codegen, before PINN retrain.

**Mechanism:** Map each subclass to Broekens explanation template (goal / enabling condition / parent plan).

**Failure mode:** Expert cannot audit LLM “intention” behind loss edit.

---

## Proposed direction 3. Epidemiologist user study with pragmatic metrics (evaluation)

**Mechanism:** Replace developer-only compliance with Miller-style usefulness/relevance ratings.

**Failure mode:** Inmates-running-asylum evaluation (Miller; Author-stated subjectivity in A).

---

## Proposed direction 4. Historicity log for rare events (data + comment context)

**Pipeline stage:** Conversational Agent + training data metadata.

**Mechanism:** Tag comments with policy/mutation context; Longo-style event register before loss edit.

**Failure mode:** Treating regime shifts as in-phase parameter noise.

---

## Conclusion

Miller says Paper A must stop evaluating XAI for researchers; Broekens says it must explain back in folk-psychological form matched to action type; Longo says expert intervention is expected when epidemic phase space shifts. Together they turn Paper A from “LLM patches PINN” into a **historically situated, contrastive, user-tested explainable agency** design — while keeping the PINN core. Paper A's own future multi-agent and template work is already aligned with this cluster; what remains is **field validation** with real epidemiologists, not more in-lab LLM benchmarks.

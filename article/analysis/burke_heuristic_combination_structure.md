> **How to read this comparison**
>
> - **Paper A** denotes Gindullina et al. (Expert-Guided PINN); **Paper B** denotes the cited source paper.
> - Quoted passages are taken from the papers named in the comparison.
> - Interpretive links between papers are argued explicitly; they are not claims made by either author team.
> - Sections titled **Proposed direction** suggest possible follow-up work. They are engineering ideas, not reported results.


Paper A (Gindullina et al., «Expert-guided forecasting of epidemic ARI incidence based on physics-informed neural networks and large language models») constrains PINN loss-function modifications through a hierarchical comment ontology and Appendix A rules (Table A.2): for each class/subclass, permitted operations such as *«Add penalty to term2»* are listed, with other terms forbidden. However, the final loss code is generated by an LLM in free form—a full Python module in one call, without a fixed representation of a «combination of modifications.» At 25–27% *«Compliance with the comment»*, the authors themselves acknowledge that the rules are insufficiently detailed and propose *class-subclass-specific modification templates*.

Paper B (Pillay, 2010, «An Empirical Study into the Structure of Heuristic Combinations in an Evolutionary Algorithm Hyper-Heuristic for the Examination Timetabling Problem») runs a controlled experiment on a related task: search in the **space of combinations** of low-level operators, not in the solution space. Two representation structures are compared: a **linear string with sequential application** (EA-LR) and a **hierarchical parse tree with simultaneous application as a function** (EA-HR). On eight benchmarks, EA-LR beats EA-HR on quality on 7/8 datasets, while the hierarchical structure is orders of magnitude slower due to parse-tree interpretation.

This yields an empirical argument for Paper A: **the form of representation for combinable rules is not neutral**—and choosing «rich» hierarchical generation (effectively what the LLM does) under the «flat» textual constraints of Table A.2 may be systematically losing. Pillay is not about epidemiology or LLMs, but her design is a rare example where representation structure is **isolated**, varied, and measured, whereas Gindullina et al. confound rule structure, LLM stochasticity, and PINN failures.

> **Quote (Pillay):** *«The main aim of this study was to empirically investigate whether the structure used for representing heuristic combinations in a heuristic space by an EA, and the application of the combinations, i.e. sequential or simultaneous, affects the performance of the EA hyper-heuristic in solving the uncapacitated examination timetabling problem.»*

---

## 1. Search in the space of combinations, not solutions

Both papers separate the layer of «how to combine primitives» from the layer of «what the final solution is.»

> **Quote (Pillay):** *«A hyper-heuristic for the examination timetabling problem searches a space of constructive heuristic combinations instead of a space of examination timetables. The most optimal heuristic combination found by the search is used to construct the examination timetable.»*

> **Quote (Gindullina et al.):** *«To ensure that LLM-generated modifications adhere to PINN principles, we restrict the space of possible loss functions via a rule-based system.»*

> **Quote (Gindullina et al.):** *«For the confirmed class and subclass combination, the corresponding loss function modification rules are extracted from the knowledge base. These rules, provided in Appendix A, represent strict guidelines for LLM, defining which components of the original loss function can be modified (e.g., by adding a penalty term) and which are prohibited.»*

In Pillay, the metaheuristic (EA) searches over a string or tree of five low-level heuristics (*l, e, w, s, h*). In Gindullina, a classifier selects an ontology branch, and the LLM searches (implicitly) in the space of Python programs over four loss terms (*term1–term4*). In both cases, **an error at the combination level** spoils the outcome (exam timetable / epidemic forecast), but Pillay measures the effect of combination representation directly; Paper A does not.

---

## 2. Two structures: linear/sequential vs hierarchical/simultaneous

The central contrast in Paper B is stated explicitly:

> **Quote (Pillay):** *«Two structures, namely, one that combines low-level construction heuristics linearly and applies them sequentially and a second which combines heuristics hierarchically and applies them simultaneously are investigated.»*

**EA-LR:** each individual is a string, e.g. *leels*; heuristics are applied in order, one per exam.

> **Quote (Pillay):** *«The heuristics are combined linearly and applied sequentially.»*

**EA-HR:** each individual is a parse tree with `if`, logical, and arithmetic operators; a function compares exam pairs and chooses ordering **simultaneously**:

> **Quote (Pillay):** *«The low-level heuristics are combined using conditional and logical operators and are applied simultaneously as part of a logical function. Each element of the population is represented using a parse tree.»*

Parallel with Paper A: Table A.2 defines a **linear catalog** of permitted primitives (*Add penalty to term2*; *Add offset to desired peak day in term2*)—closer to EA-LR. LLM generation, by contrast, produces **arbitrary executable code** with nested conditionals, reassignment of boundary-condition weights, etc.—expressiveness at the EA-HR level, but without genetic search over valid trees, without a separate interpretation phase, and with the risk of semantic mismatch with the comment.

---

## 3. Empirical finding: representation determines success

Pillay's main result is that structure is **not interchangeable**:

> **Quote (Pillay):** *«The study has revealed that the representation used does have an impact on the success of the evolutionary algorithm. In this domain the linear combination and sequential application of heuristics produced better results.»*

> **Quote (Pillay):** *«The EA-LR has produced better quality timetables for seven of the eight data sets.»*

> **Quote (Pillay):** *«It is evident from this study that the structure does have an effect and an EA representing heuristics combinations linearly and applying them sequentially appears to perform better than an EA with a representation combining heuristics hierarchically and applying them simultaneously.»*

For Gindullina, this means: before complicating modification templates or adding multi-agent LLMs, one must **empirically fix** the representation class for loss modifications. Today the system declares «flat» rules but delegates «trees» to the LLM—an internal contradiction that Pillay, in another domain, would show as metric degradation.

Additionally, hierarchy is costlier not only in quality but in computation:

> **Quote (Pillay):** *«The runtimes for the EA-HR are much higher than that for the EA-LR. This can be attributed to the fact that in the EA-HR each heuristic combination has to be interpreted and evaluated whereas in the EA-LR this is not the case as the heuristics are applied in order sequentially instead of simultaneously as a function.»*

> **Quote (Pillay):** *«Furthermore, more processing time will be needed by the mutation and crossover operation when dealing with a hierarchical structure compared to a simple string representing an individual.»*

Analog for PINN Customizer: each «hierarchical» LLM edit requires full PINN retraining and iterative LLM debug calls—the cost of interpreting free-form code is already recorded by Gindullina as *«significantly increases computational costs and time»* with low initial compilability of the 8B model.

Pillay's earlier work (2008) compared **three linear** representations; the present study is the first step toward hierarchy in the same domain and immediately records a regression:

> **Quote (Pillay):** *«Previous work has shown that the representation used by an evolutionary algorithm affects the success of the algorithm.»*

> **Quote (Pillay):** *«The study presented in this paper takes this a step further and performs an empirical comparison of the linear structure with the sequential application of low-level heuristics to a hierarchical structure which applies the low-level heuristics simultaneously.»*

---

## 4. «Hierarchical» classifier ≠ hierarchy of loss modification

Paper A uses the word *hierarchical* for the **comment taxonomy** (classes 1–4 and subclasses in Fig. A.6), but the modification rules in Table A.2 are a flat list of alternatives per subclass, without a parse tree or compositional semantics.

> **Quote (Gindullina et al.):** *«Each class was divided into a set of more specific subclasses, covering the main variations in expert formulations (Appendix A).»*

This is orthogonal to Pillay's question: there, hierarchy is a property of the **genotype** in heuristic space, not of task labels. Conflating levels in Gindullina creates an illusion of structural control: the expert confirms a comment-ontology node, but the LLM can still generate a hierarchically complex program not isomorphic to any Table A.2 template—hence cases where modification *«may be effectively random rather than logically justified by epidemiological reasoning»*.

---

## 5. Table A.2 as weak EA-LR; LLM output as uncontrolled EA-HR

Table A.2 (fragment):

| Class | Permitted Modifications | Constraints |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| 1 | Add penalty to term2 / term1 / term4 / term3 | Modification of other function components is **prohibited** |
| 4 | Add offset to desired peak day in term2 | Modification of other function components is **prohibited** |

This is an **alphabet** and a discrete set of strings, not executable semantics: the word *penalty* does not specify form, weight, or range. Pillay for EA-LR at least fixes that *«each heuristic is used to schedule one examination»* and how the string cycles—predictable application mechanics.

When Llama-70B, in response to a peak shift, *«reducing the influence of the zero boundary condition»* (Fig. 4b), that is not a Table A.2 syntax violation in the narrow sense (term2 may be untouched), but a violation of the **sequential, interpretable** scheme of «one permitted operation on one term»—behavior closer to an arbitrary EA-HR tree. A similar ambiguous case (Fig. 4c): fourfold strengthening of the boundary condition when asked to continue growth—a composition of operators not reducible to a single Table A.2 string. The authors note:

> **Quote (Gindullina et al.):** *«This example illustrates that, in the absence of clear and interpretable modification rules, an LLM may generate a loss function whose use leads to inadequate forecasts.»*

In Pillay's terms: a more expressive representation structure was chosen without empirical justification that it is better for the domain—and the domain punishes (700+ epidemic days).

---

## 6. Future templates: lesson—choose and compare representations first

Paper A plans to replace crude penalties with templates:

> **Quote (Gindullina et al.):** *«We plan to replace the simple penalty additions by class-subclass-specific modification templates. These templates will explicitly define which loss components and parameters can be altered, which mathematical operators are admissible, and what numerical ranges are permitted.»*

Pillay sets a methodological precedent for that choice: **two explicit structures**, the same primitive set, the same fitness, 10 runs on 8 instances, comparison with other hyper-heuristics (Appendix A, Table 9). Recommendation for Gindullina: when implementing templates, run a Pillay-style ablation—(A) linear sequence of template operators, (B) hierarchical composition (e.g. if-then by epidemic phase)—on the same 20 comments and same LLMs, before fixing the architecture.

Pillay herself leaves statistical significance testing open—but already demands not ignoring representation:

> **Quote (Pillay):** *«As this study is empirical in nature future work will test the significance of this result and identify reasons for the difference in performance of both the structures.»*

Paper A has not yet isolated any structural factor (only LLM scale is compared under an identical one-shot architecture).

## 8. Directions for the Gindullina team

**Direction 1. Fix modification genotype as EA-LR.** For each subclass—a short **linear** sequence of typed operators (e.g. `[INCREASE_PENALTY term2 slope_post_peak bounds=(0.1,2.0)]`), applied deterministically, without free if/else in LLM code. This directly implements modification templates and reduces the risk of arbitrary boundary-condition changes (Fig. 4b).

**Direction 2. If conditional logic is needed—a separate EA-HR ablation.** Any hierarchical composition of templates (pre-/post-peak phase) must be compared with linear on held-out comments by compliance and WIS—following Pillay's protocol (10+ runs, same instances). Without such a test, «rich» representation should not be the default.

**Direction 3. Decouple comment hierarchy from code hierarchy.** The BERT classifier remains a hierarchical index into the knowledge base; PINN Customizer output is only the selected linear template element + parameters from NL (with ranges from templates). Mixing levels (hierarchical labels + flat rule text + hierarchical code) is the current architectural defect.

**Direction 4. Account for interpretation cost.** As EA-HR loses on runtime due to parse trees, free LLM code increases debug iterations and full PINN-retrain cycles. Linear templates reduce both semantic and computational losses—measurable end-to-end metrics (time to forecast, number of LLM calls). Pillay states a general hyper-heuristic principle: the goal is *«generalize well rather than producing the best result for one or more problem sets»*—for Gindullina, this argues for testing modification representation across multiple waves/regions, not only on one St. Petersburg COVID dataset.

---

## Summary

Pillay (2010) empirically shows that when searching the space of **combinations** of low-level operators, linear sequential representation beats hierarchical functional—both in quality and cost.

> **Quote (Pillay):** *«The main contribution of the study presented in this paper is an empirical investigation into whether the structure used by an EA to represent heuristic combinations in a heuristic space and the application of the heuristics in the combinations affects the performance of the EA.»*

Expert-Guided PINN declares flat Table A.2 rules but assigns the LLM to generate code of unbounded structure; this reproduces the losing EA-HR branch without a controlled experiment. Essay 11 (Gruber) gave the ontological blueprint for templates; Pillay adds the **experimental design**: before implementing templates, one must choose and measure **representation structure**, not only prompt content.
